What it does

Built for people who watch pages for a living

⏱

Live countdown timer

A bold countdown ring shows exactly how long until the next refresh — not just a number, a visual timer that ticks down in real time. Plus a running total of how many refreshes have fired.

📲

Telegram alerts when it needs you

Connect your own Telegram bot and get a message the moment a tab needs a human: a captcha, a “verify you’re human” wall, or a word you’re watching for. The message names the account, so you know which one. No more alerts for every small page change.

⚡

Any interval you want

Six one-tap presets from 5 seconds to 15 minutes, plus a custom field in seconds, minutes, or hours.

🗂

Multiple tabs at once

Refresh your dashboard every 30 seconds and a release page every 5 minutes. Each tab keeps its own timer, options, and schedule.

🔨

Hard refresh option

Toggle on bypass-cache mode to force a true fresh load every cycle. Some dashboards serve stale data from cache — this makes sure you're always seeing the real thing.

📅

Up to 7 time windows a day

Set the exact hours a tab should be live — say 3am–5am, 7am–12pm and 2pm–7pm. It refreshes inside each window, stops the second one ends, and starts itself again when the next one opens. Pick days of the week too.

🌎

Its own US time zone

The schedule runs on one fixed time zone — US Eastern by default, or Central, Mountain, Arizona, Pacific, Alaska or Hawaii — not your computer's clock. 3am Eastern means 3am Eastern wherever you are, daylight saving included.

🔄

Sync across your computers

Optional free account: log in once and your time windows, intervals and options follow you to every computer where Auto Refresh is installed. Change something on one and it appears on the others. Skip it and nothing ever leaves your browser.

✨

Lynch AI can set it up for you

A chat tab right inside the extension. Tell it what you want — "set it up for 3am to 5am and 7am to 12pm" — and it makes the change itself, no clicking required. Ask a question instead and it just answers. It never sees what page you're on.

⌨️

Keyboard shortcuts

Press Ctrl+Shift+R to instantly start or stop auto-refresh on whatever tab you're on. Press Ctrl+Shift+P to pause or resume. No popup needed.

⏸

Per-tab pause

Need to interact with a page manually? Hit pause — the timer holds without losing your settings. Resume when you're done and the countdown picks right back up.

🔒

Private by default

No account needed, and nothing about the pages you refresh ever leaves your browser. Your settings stay in your browser unless you choose to log in to sync them. Once a day it checks for a new version and sends an anonymous install count (a random ID and the version number, nothing else), and both can be switched off in the popup. Otherwise it only goes online when you set something up or start it: “Check for updates”, chatting with Lynch AI, syncing after you log in, or sending alerts to your own Telegram bot.

🎯

Survives sleep

Built on Chrome's alarms system, so timers re-arm themselves after the browser idles or restarts.

📊

Countdown on the icon

The toolbar icon counts down the seconds to the next refresh — 300, 299, 298 — for whichever tab you’re on. Gold while refreshing, grey while waiting for the next window. Want it bigger? Switch on the on-page timer and it sits in the top-right corner of the page.

🎰

Random interval ranges

Give it a window instead of a number — 250s to 300s — and every single refresh waits a fresh random amount inside that range. A page seeing reloads at 271s, 288s, 254s looks nothing like a script on a fixed timer.

🎲

Random jitter

Adds a ±10% random delay to each refresh cycle so perfectly-timed reloads don't trigger bot detection. Looks human, stays reliable.

✋

Pauses itself, then carries on

When a tab needs a human it stops reloading, so a captcha isn’t reset while you solve it. The toolbar icon shows a red !, the popup says why, and a chime plays if you want one. Fix the page and it starts refreshing again by itself.

🖱️

Acts human on the page

Optional. After each refresh it moves the mouse along curved paths, scrolls a little and back, and now and then clicks empty space, never a link or a button. Every visit is different, so a tab looks lived in instead of reloading on a timer. Off by default.

⏳

Session timer

See total elapsed time since you started auto-refreshing a tab, right alongside the refresh count. "Running for 3h 22m, 402 refreshes."

Install in about 60 seconds

Load it into Chrome

Works in Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and any Chromium browser.

  1. 1

    Download and unzip

    Grab lynch-digitance-auto-refresh.zip and unzip it. You'll get a folder with manifest.json inside.

    Download .zip
  2. 2

    Open the extensions page

    Type chrome://extensions in your address bar and press Enter.

  3. 3

    Turn on Developer mode

    Flip the Developer mode toggle in the top-right corner of that page.

  4. 4

    Load unpacked

    Click Load unpacked, then select the unzipped folder. The Lynch Digitance logo appears in your toolbar.

  5. 5

    Start refreshing

    Open any page, click the logo, choose an interval and hit Start auto refresh.

Already installed?

Updating takes about 10 seconds

You never have to remove the extension. Overwrite the folder, hit reload, and every interval and schedule you have saved stays exactly where it is.

  1. 1

    Unzip over your existing folder

    Download the new .zip and unzip it into the same folder you loaded last time (the one with manifest.json directly inside), replacing the old files when asked.

    Download latest
  2. 2

    Click reload on the card

    Open chrome://extensions and press the circular reload ↻ icon on the Auto Refresh card. That is the whole update.

  3. 3

    Check the version

    The card should now read 2.5.5. Your saved settings are already back in the popup.

Keep the same folder. Chrome ties your saved settings to where the extension lives. Unzipping to a new spot (like a second copy in Downloads) registers a fresh extension with empty settings — and deleting and reinstalling wipes them for the same reason. Overwriting in place is what keeps everything.

Questions

Good to know

Why Developer mode instead of the Chrome Web Store?

This is your own build, delivered directly to you. Loading unpacked is the standard way to run a private extension. If you ever want it listed publicly on the Chrome Web Store, that needs a one-time developer account with Google — just say the word and we can plan it.

Can Lynch AI actually change my settings, or just explain them?

It changes them. Tell it what you want — "set it up for 3am to 5am and 7am to 12pm", "add a window from 8pm to 10pm", "switch to Pacific time", "start refreshing this tab" — and it does it right there, no clicking required. It confirms exactly what it changed underneath its reply. Ask it a question instead ("how do time windows work?") and it just answers, nothing is touched.

How do I know when there's a new version?

Open the popup and click Check for updates at the bottom — it compares the version you're running against the latest one published here and tells you either way. Unpacked extensions can't auto-update themselves, so nothing is ever installed behind your back; the button just tells you when it's worth grabbing the new zip.

How do I update without losing my settings?

Unzip the new download over the same folder you loaded before, then click the reload ↻ icon on the extension's card at chrome://extensions. Your intervals and schedules are kept. Don't delete and reinstall — that clears them. There's a step-by-step above.

Will it keep refreshing if I close the popup?

Yes. The popup is just the control panel. Timers live in the extension's background worker, so you can close the popup and even switch tabs freely.

Can I refresh faster than every 30 seconds?

Yes. Chrome's alarm system has a 30-second floor, so anything shorter (like the 5s and 15s presets) is driven by a chained timer instead — 5 seconds really means 5 seconds.

What time zone does the schedule follow?

Its own, never your computer's. Pick one of seven US zones in the popup — Eastern is the default. The popup shows the live time in that zone, so you can see exactly what it's working from. Daylight saving changes are handled for you. If you use an account, the time zone stays on each computer and is not synced.

What happens between my time windows?

It sleeps. The countdown — in the popup and on the toolbar icon — switches to the seconds until the next window opens, shown in grey. When the window opens it refreshes straight away and carries on — you don't need to touch anything. A window can cross midnight, like 10pm to 2am.

What counts as “needs a human touch”?

Two things. First, the page is blocking or challenging you: a captcha or “verify you’re human” check, a “press and hold” wall, a “checking your browser” page, or a short access denied or too many requests page. Second, one of your own words appears on the page, like “new message” or “action required”. It reads text that is already on the page, inside the tab, after every refresh. That catches the usual walls, but it can occasionally miss an unusual one or flag a normal page. You can switch the blocked check off and rely on your own words.

How do I connect Telegram?

In Telegram, message @BotFather, send /newbot and copy the token it gives you. In the popup, tick “Alert me when it needs a human touch”, open the Telegram section and paste the token. Then send your new bot any message in Telegram (bots can’t message you first), press Detect chat ID, and press Save. A confirmation arrives in Telegram. It takes about two minutes.

Is my Telegram bot token safe?

If you’re not logged in, the token stays on that computer only. If you are logged in, the connection (bot token and chat ID) is also saved to your account so your other computers can alert you too. It is encrypted before it is stored, with a key kept apart from the database, so a copy of the database alone shows nothing readable. It is not end-to-end encrypted, because our server holds the key so it can hand the connection back to your own computers. The token is never shown again after you save it, Lynch AI can’t see it, and the chat refuses to send anything that looks like a bot token. Alerts go straight from your browser to Telegram. Disconnect removes it from the computer and from your account. Prefer it on one computer only? Don’t log in, or connect the bot on that computer while logged out.

Why did my tab pause and show a red “!”?

When a tab needs a human it pauses on purpose, because reloading a captcha would reset it while you’re solving it. The popup says why. Fix the page and it carries on by itself a few seconds after the problem is gone (you can also press Resume, or Ctrl+Shift+P, any time). If you’d rather it kept going, switch off “Pause this tab until it’s fixed”: it then alerts once and reminds you every 30 minutes.

What does “Act human on the page” do, and can it beat bot protection?

It is a switch in the popup, off by default. After each refresh, while the next one counts down, the extension plays a short visit inside the page: the mouse moves along curved, uneven paths, the page scrolls a little and returns to where it was, and about every other visit it clicks once on empty space. A second tick box turns the clicks off. It never clicks a link, button, field, image or menu, never while a dialog is open, and it stops the moment you touch the page yourself. It reads nothing from the page and sends nothing anywhere. What it helps with: sites that log you out when they see no activity, and simple activity checks. What it can’t do: beat real bot protection such as Cloudflare or captcha systems. Browsers mark events made by an extension as not coming from a real device, and those systems look at far more than the mouse. If a captcha or block page does appear, Auto Refresh leaves the page alone, pauses, and alerts you so you can handle it.

Do I need an account?

No. The account is optional and only exists to sync your settings between computers. Skip it and Auto Refresh works exactly as before, and nothing is sent to the account server.

How does syncing work?

Open the Account tab in the popup, sign up or log in, and your saved settings (interval, random range, time windows and options) are kept on your account. Change something on one computer and it is sent within a couple of seconds. Your other computers pick it up when you open the popup, when the browser starts, every 15 minutes, or when you press Sync now. Tabs that are already refreshing keep the settings they started with. Logging in on a new computer loads what is saved on your account. Your time zone and account name are not synced: every computer keeps its own, so you can set them separately. A Telegram bot you have connected does sync (stored encrypted), so every computer can alert you.

What does an account store, and how do I delete it?

Your email, a scrambled (salted and hashed) copy of your password, and your saved settings. Never the pages you refresh, their addresses or their contents. To delete it, open the Account tab, choose Delete my account and confirm with your password. The account and its stored settings are removed; the settings on your computer stay.

I forgot my password. Can I reset it?

Not yet. There is no reset email in this version, so a lost password can't be recovered. You can sign up again with a new account, and the settings on your computer are untouched. As a safeguard, six wrong passwords in a row lock an account for 15 minutes.

Does it collect any of my data?

Not by default. Your intervals and schedules are saved in Chrome's local storage on your own machine, and nothing about the pages you refresh is tracked. Once a day the extension checks for a new version and sends an anonymous install count (a random install ID and the version number, never your email, settings or pages) so the developer can count how many people use it; two tick boxes in the popup switch these off. Beyond that, the extension only goes online when you set something up or start it yourself: “Check for updates” downloads a small public file with the latest version number; a Lynch AI chat message is sent with a summary of your refresh settings so it can answer or make the change you asked for; if you choose to log in, your settings sync to your account (it stores your email, a scrambled copy of your password and your saved settings and, if you connected one, your Telegram bot connection, encrypted); and if you connect a Telegram bot, alerts go straight from your browser to Telegram and your own bot. The address and title of the page you're refreshing are never sent to us, and page contents are never sent anywhere. An alert shows your account name and the page's address (the site and path, never anything after a question mark). If you didn't type a name it uses the page's title. These go only to your own Telegram. Switch the two daily checks off and use none of those, and it never goes online.

What happens when I close a watched tab?

Its timer is removed automatically, so nothing is left running in the background.
